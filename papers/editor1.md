The manuscript proposes an architecture to execute distributed data engineering and machine learning (ML) operations across hybrid environments, including High-Performance Computing (HPC) clusters and serverless cloud platforms. The authors introduce Cylon, a high-performance distributed dataframe library. To overcome the performance bottlenecks of traditional serverless communication (often mediated by external storage such as AWS S3), the study develops a custom serverless communicator based on NAT traversal / TCP hole punching.

Empirically, the study evaluates a distributed Join operator under weak- and strong-scaling configurations. The results suggest that the serverless communicator enables AWS Lambda to achieve performance comparable to serverful AWS EC2 instances and an HPC cluster (Rivanna), with a reported difference on the order of <1% in strong-scaling experiments.

Strengths
1. The work targets portability: the same distributed dataframe/data-engineering workload is evaluated across HPC and cloud, and containerization helps maintain consistency across environments.
2. he integration of UCX/UCC for non-serverless platforms is a meaningful step toward bringing HPC-grade communication into data-engineering/ML pipelines.
3/ The paper evaluates an end-to-end, communication-heavy operator (distributed Join) under both weak and strong scaling across multiple environments (EC2, Lambda, HPC).
4. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges current constraints (operator coverage, Lambda's 15-minute cap, lack of checkpointing/fault tolerance) and outlines future directions.

Limitations and revision requests
1. Contributions are not clearly articulated. Please add a concise "Contributions" subsection that distinguishes: (i) what is reused/adapted from FMI/Moyer, (ii) what is new in communicator semantics/APIs (e.g., collectives required by join/shuffle), (iii) what is new in orchestration/runtime integration (e.g., Step Functions workflow), and (iv) what is new in evaluation/cross-platform portability.
2. Evaluation scope is limited relative to the stated ML goal. The title suggests support for "ML data-intensive applications," yet the experiments focus exclusively on Join. To justify the broader claim, please include additional representative ML preprocessing/data-engineering tasks (e.g., groupby/aggregation, feature scaling/normalization, sampling) and/or an end-to-end training/inference pipeline if Cylon integrates with PyTorch/TensorFlow as stated. Evaluating only Join (an all-to-all heavy operator) does not cover other common communication patterns (e.g., Reduce/AllReduce, AllGather) that are central to many ML workflows.
3. Missing baseline comparisons to isolate the proposed contribution. A direct comparison against (i) "stock" FMI (if applicable) and/or (ii) a storage-mediated serverless baseline (e.g., S3-based shuffle) would better quantify the benefit of the proposed communicator and any Cylon-specific additions.
4. Cost analysis is not supported by the presented experiments. While cost is a major motivation for serverless computing, the paper references large cost reductions (e.g., 397×) but does not provide a cost breakdown for the experiments in this manuscript. Please include a cost model based on the actual runs (Lambda GB-s, Step Functions transitions, rendezvous/server cost, and EC2 hourly rates for equivalent throughput).

Clarifications needed
1. The manuscript should define precisely what the "<1%" figure refers to (e.g., relative runtime difference, speedup difference, scaling efficiency) and at which scale points.
2. The Rivanna 1-node runtime is faster than EC2 1-node runtime, which suggests compute differences may confound comparisons of scaling behavior. Please provide a breakdown of total time into compute vs communication and orchestration overhead, or add a communication-focused microbenchmark, to support conclusions about communication performance across environments.
